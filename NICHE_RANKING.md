## Watch out for

- **Copyright on match footage.** ATP/WTA/Grand Slam broadcast clips get Content ID claims. Winning channels use stills, animation, stats graphics and commentary. Your own court footage is a big advantage for technique demonstrations.
- **Seasonality.** Sports views spike around the Slams (Jan, May–Jun, Jul, Aug–Sep). Time uploads before each one.

## r/YouTubeCreators takeaways (read 2026-10-25)

Source: the top 25 posts of the month plus 7 main threads.

**About the "$3k in 7 weeks" post:** OP sends the niche only by DM and calls the production tool "a private tool". The proof is a Drive folder of screenshots, which are easy to fake (see the 437-upvote "Stop Believing Folks Posting Their Channel Stats" thread). Likely a lead-in to selling the tool. The method is sound, though: real-world video and images, stock footage and animation, with AI only speeding up production. Build it yourself instead of buying it.

**Lessons**
1. **Long videos and watch time.** 15–25 minute videos win. Examples: a 21-minute video with 45% average watched got 100K views in 3 days, and a first channel shared numbers from a 25–30 minute video. Older audiences watch longer. A 20-minute video that holds viewers 6 minutes beats a 5-minute video that holds them 3.
2. **Put the heaviest editing in the first half** of the video. Lighter editing after that saves days per video.
3. **Brand-new channels can go viral from day one** with: heavily searched title words, simple thumbnails that read on a phone, and broad-appeal topics (brain and psychology). "Shadow bans don't exist; your videos just aren't good yet."
4. **Make the title and thumbnail separate ideas.** The title states the topic; the thumbnail makes the viewer think "what?"
5. **The trend cycle:** one creator goes viral, copycats fill the gaps, and teams with budgets test many angles. You only see the survivors. Use the outlier finder to catch a format early.
6. **The $800k creator (no AI, no Shorts, one person):** put out a lot of videos fast, study the analytics, repeat what works. Find your own lane in the niche ("blue ocean"). Pick a topic you'd cover even without pay. 80% of your best is fine.
7. **AI content:** viewers don't care whether it's AI-made if it's good. What fails is AI videos where the visuals don't match the narration.

**⚠️ Channel safety**
- YouTube's related-channel policy can demonetize your channel if anyone with access (an editor or manager) had a channel demonetized before. Even a shared IP address has triggered it.
- **Never give freelancers channel access.** Have them send the exported files and upload them yourself.
- Keep each channel's Google account clean, and don't reuse accounts tied to old reused-content strikes.
